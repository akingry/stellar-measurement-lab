How the percentages work

Percentages are relative fit shares among scored model stages, not calibrated probabilities of true evolutionary stage. Equal starting weights do not represent stellar population frequencies. Each stage contributes its best-fitting supported solution; its likelihood is divided by the sum across supported stages. This profile-likelihood approach avoids grid-point counting, but does not integrate over mass, age or composition priors. It is not a Bayesian posterior.

Uncalibrated solutions remain possible. Shares conditional on the calibrated subset must not be interpreted as excluding them. Entirely unsupported stages show “Not scored.” A high share does not guarantee a good absolute fit. Magnesium measurements beyond five assumed standard deviations from every tested solution are flagged as conflicting.

Adaptive controls and selection

Temperature and luminosity appear first. Additional measurement options appear only for overlapping stages with an applicable diagnostic. Enable multiple measurements to calculate one combined relative fit. Their likelihoods are multiplied for each individual candidate solution before taking the best joint solution for each stage and normalizing. Separate stage percentages are not multiplied or averaged. Every selected measurement must be supported for a solution to be scored. Missing calibrations do not count as zero likelihood. This approximation assumes conditional independence of measurement errors at fixed model parameters; shared temperature, atmosphere, abundance and calibration systematics are not modeled. It is not an empirically validated joint posterior. Seismic density remains a catalog class-level factor, not an individual oscillation prediction. Single-measurement colors remain unchanged. Combined spectral residuals above a mean squared standardized residual of 25 across all selected spectral measurements are flagged as conflicting if no tested solution passes. Slider changes clear the preview. Any listed candidate can be selected or reselected. Selecting a lower-fit or untested candidate does not change its percentage or imply that the measurements favor it. The star remains hidden until selection.

Lithium

Blend-corrected lithium 6708 angstrom equivalent width is measured in milliangstroms. EAGLES neural predictions and intrinsic dispersion are sampled into a temperature-age table. A five milliangstrom measurement uncertainty and exact temperature are assumed. Only young and main-sequence solutions at 3000–6500 kelvin, ages two million to six billion years, and initial iron composition −0.3 to +0.2 are tested. Gaussian likelihoods include intrinsic scatter and normalization. Mapping grid ages to this empirical relation is a cross-model assumption, not independent validation. Initial composition is not necessarily current observed abundance.

Magnesium

The slider uses an expanded, piecewise-linear nonlinear display scale. Crossovers are evenly spaced and candidate fit peaks are given additional space. The numeric readout remains the physical flux index; the full 0 to 0.7 domain and the likelihood calculation are unchanged.

The flux index uses 585 BOSZ atmosphere spectra, the linked passbands, fixed atmospheric assumptions, and an assumed Gaussian flux-index uncertainty of 0.01. Systematic atmosphere errors are not calibrated. Mass and radius determine gravity within the forward prediction; gravity is not a user input. Only previously supported phases and atmosphere combinations are scored. Missing phases remain untested.

Oscillations

Each oscillation slider shows the two catalog-class fits with the other two oscillation values held fixed. Orange denotes shell-burning giants and green core helium burning. Markers locate equal-class fits, not boundaries between every evolutionary phase. Gray lies outside calibration; some fixed frequency combinations have no crossover. These individual colors do not include spectrum or lithium. The overall ranking still combines enabled data. Shell-burning colours cannot distinguish red giants from different asymptotic giant stages.

Radial frequency spacing, peak frequency and dipole period spacing describe brightness variability. Gaussian kernel densities use 3,046 training catalog stars, with Scott bandwidths in log frequency spacing, log peak frequency and period spacing. The training convex hull limits use. Withheld stars were not used to fit the densities. Earlier decision-tree tests do not validate percentages from this different estimator.

The catalog combines red and asymptotic giants in one class; core helium burning is the other. Shell-burning likelihood is applied identically to red, early asymptotic and thermally pulsing candidates, with equal per-stage starting weights. Those stages remain tied. These densities are conditional on catalog selection, not individual seismic models at the selected temperature and luminosity. Reference labels share seismic information. They are exploratory fit shares, not independently calibrated physical probabilities.

Coverage and visual

Included nonrotating MIST stages use same-phase interpolation at the exact selected temperature and luminosity, without a tolerance box or nearest-star fallback. Source labels combining stages remain combined. No match means missing grid coverage, not physical impossibility. First load downloads about 24 megabytes of evolutionary data plus diagnostic tables.

Temperature is light-derived; intrinsic luminosity additionally needs distance and extinction when inferred observationally. Radius follows the Stefan–Boltzmann law. The selected star's colour and radius depend on temperature and luminosity, not a claimed view of its interior. Animation is illustrative. Displayed mass represents one fitted model, not a unique measured mass.

Not all stellar types or stages are covered. Hot-star subclasses, binary evolution, detailed remnant atmospheres and uniquely resolved late giants remain outside this classifier. Technetium is omitted from ranking because no stage-likelihood calibration was established.

Recorded-star fitting (version 25)

Recorded stars with usable temperature and radius errors are evaluated over the native evolutionary grid, using their measured values and Gaussian error likelihoods. Radius and temperature are the fitting coordinates when luminosity was derived from them, avoiding independent use of the same luminosity twice. Asymmetric radius errors are retained. Where only temperature and luminosity errors exist, those coordinates are used. Covariance is unavailable in these records, so zero covariance is an explicit approximation. The numerical search is limited to five quoted errors in each coordinate. Cooling grids or records without sufficient errors retain exact-position inference; this fallback is displayed.

Grid-cell weights integrate over initial mass and linear age, with a Kroupa initial-mass density, constant formation rate over the supplied age grid and uniform initial metallicity over the supplied grid. These are explicit population assumptions, not measurements or catalog-label information. Model-weighted support is not an independently calibrated probability, and should not be interpreted as a universal stellar-population posterior. The grids remain single-star, nonrotating models.

All recorded usable diagnostics are enabled. A diagnostic only refines the relative weights inside its calibrated domain: the total support assigned to that domain is retained, so a missing likelihood cannot silently exclude another stage. The fixed update order is magnesium, lithium, oscillations. These conditional updates are not a full generative joint likelihood across all evolutionary stages. Period-spacing errors are integrated with three-point Gaussian quadrature; uncalibrated uncertainty tails retain their previous support instead of extrapolated evidence or discarding the whole measurement. Raw frequency errors and parameter covariance are not available in the stored records. Lithium is never extrapolated to evolved stars. Evolved candidates now retain their measured-position support instead of receiving no score. Slider colors still show the individual central-position comparison, not the full error-integrated result.

Population weighting and likelihood-based uncertainty fitting are established approaches; see BASTA and PARAM. The conditional updates described above are this app's conservative handling of incomplete diagnostic coverage, not a claim that those tools implement the same method.

Real-star Random test

Sample, measurement coverage, database downloads and comparison limits. Recorded lithium uncertainties are used when loading catalog data. Temperature and radius errors are now used where available; incomplete error and cooling coverage is explicitly labeled.
