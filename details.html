Evidence and limits

Version eleven keeps all light-diagnostic sliders visible and connects them to published measurements. These are separate diagnostic estimates, not a joint fit of temperature, luminosity, spectrum and stellar interior. An arbitrary combination of sliders is not guaranteed to describe a possible star. The illustration and radius depend only on temperature and luminosity; additional controls update the evidence, not an invented interior.

Candidate stages and guidance

The star is above the diagram. The Sun-like starting point is labelled with pre-main-sequence and main-sequence candidates from exact-position interpolation in the included grid. The guide explains that the current giant diagnostics cannot distinguish these young versus mature possibilities; a different calibrated youth diagnostic would be required.

Candidate stages use the existing same-phase, piecewise-linear interpolation at the selected temperature and luminosity across all eleven included composition grids. There is no surrounding tolerance box or nearest-row fallback. Multiple solutions remain visible, and no match means absent coverage at that position, not physical impossibility. The first load downloads approximately 24 megabytes. Source labels that group stages remain grouped.

Contextual guidance explains directions for period spacing and the paired technetium blend measurements. Try buttons change explicit light quantities, not a stage identifier. Period-spacing suggestions are checked against the existing measurement envelope and preserve the chosen frequency pair when possible. Fallback combinations are published catalog examples and use zero period-spacing uncertainty to explore their central values; this is not a claim of error-free observations. Both methods leave temperature and luminosity unchanged. Explore-point buttons explicitly move temperature and luminosity to a demonstration location; they do not resolve ambiguity at the original position.

Changing illustrative measurements explores hypotheses; it is not a recommendation to alter actual observations. Actual classification requires measuring the additional quantities. Candidate lists are not automatically filtered by separate, incompletely joined diagnostics. The presence of an evolved model candidate does not itself prove that the cool-giant diagnostic calibration applies. See young-star oscillation evidence for the distinct early-evolution branch.

Brightness oscillations

The controls specify radial frequency spacing and the frequency of maximum oscillation power in microhertz, and fitted asymptotic dipole gravity-mode period spacing in seconds. These are extracted from a long-duration brightness time series with identified oscillation modes. They are not interchangeable with one ordinary pulsation period.

A depth-four decision tree was fitted to 3,046 training stars from the joined 2016 measured-spacing and 2025 consensus catalogs. It reproduces the saved predictions on 1,016 withheld stars, with zero disagreements in that particular test. The reference labels use related seismic information, so this is not independent proof of physical stages, a probability of correctness, or a universal accuracy claim. The catalog's combined red-giant/asymptotic-giant category remains combined.

The browser refuses extrapolation outside the convex envelope of the training measurements, using logarithmic frequency coordinates and linear period spacing. An envelope is an interpolation-domain guard, not a proof that every point within it is populated or physically possible. The period-spacing uncertainty slider specifies a symmetric interval, not a fitted posterior or guaranteed confidence level. If the interval crosses the tree's label boundary, the output remains overlapping. If any of its center or endpoints lies outside the envelope, the output is outside calibration. Frequency uncertainties and other systematic errors are not propagated. The estimate is conditional on accurately measured frequencies and the applicable oscillating-giant population.

Technetium spectrum

The two sliders specify central wavelengths of blended absorption features near 4238 and 4262 angstroms in the stellar rest frame. They are not abundances or equivalent widths. Convex envelopes of the paired measured wavelengths are computed separately for technetium-rich and technetium-poor reference groups in 64 published M- and S-family stars. The two measurements must jointly fall within one envelope; a gap or unsupported pairing remains outside calibration. This is an in-sample spectral-pattern comparison, not a separately validated stage classifier.

The source is the version-one author preprint, Tables 4 and 5, retaining its numerical precision. Per-feature uncertainties are not available in those tables. The displayed digits and slider step are numerical controls, not a claim of instrumental accuracy. Real spectra require matching wavelength convention, velocity correction, resolution and blending treatment. These controls are only relevant to comparable cool evolved M/S spectra, not all temperatures displayed on the diagram.

A technetium-rich pattern supports recent dredge-up of processed material. It does not establish an exact thermal-pulse position or a unique current stage. A technetium-poor pattern does not rule out thermally pulsing evolution. Neither technetium result overrides the oscillation result; disagreeing diagnostics require further interpretation rather than a forced answer.

Temperature, luminosity and the visual

Radius follows the Stefan–Boltzmann law with solar luminosity 3.828 × 10²⁶ watts and solar radius 6.957 × 10⁸ metres. Temperature is a spectrum-derived effective temperature, and luminosity is intrinsic bolometric luminosity, requiring distance and extinction information when inferred from observations. The explorer treats those derived values as supplied; it does not process raw observations. No mass, age, gravity or stage is an input, and no mass is inferred in this version.

The diagram shows sampled nonrotating evolutionary grids across the included compositions, with later phases. Temperature and luminosity are fitted to this grid; the additional diagnostic controls are not part of a joint fit. The source combines post-asymptotic-giant and white-dwarf cooling labels. Its presence in the background does not imply those stages are classified. Surface colour is a blackbody approximation; surface motion and compressed radius display are illustrative, not a hydrodynamic or evolutionary calculation.

What is not implemented

Hot-star line-ratio classifications, later-giant acoustic-pattern fits, thermal-pulse period-change fits, white-dwarf atmosphere classifications and an all-stage joint model are not calibrated here. They have not been replaced with arbitrary cutoffs. The unused magnesium slider from version nine has been removed. Controls are independent and never snap to a secretly selected star. Reset supplies illustrative measurement values. All sliders remain visible; diagnostics outside the relevant candidate families are marked as not applicable.
