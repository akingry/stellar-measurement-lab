Assumptions and sources

One selected model estimate is shown, not a uniquely measured stellar history. This is a constrained model explorer, not an observational fitting or uncertainty tool. Moving any slider requests a value; all three controls snap together to a supported model and display its actual values (rounded for readability). Values may change substantially, and gaps in model coverage are not evidence that a star cannot exist.

Light-only inputs

The three control quantities are effective temperature inferred from the spectrum or color, intrinsic bolometric luminosity inferred from light with a fixed distance and extinction assumption, and continuum-normalized spectral absorption. No mass, age, gravitational acceleration or evolutionary stage is an input or a hidden slider coordinate. Those properties belong to the selected model, not independent measurements.

For this illustrative explorer, adopt a fixed fiducial distance of ten parsecs, zero interstellar extinction, isotropic emission and an isolated single star. Intrinsic luminosity equals four times pi times distance squared times extinction-corrected bolometric flux. At this fixed distance each luminosity corresponds to a flux; the slider displays intrinsic luminosity in solar units, never raw apparent flux. This is a documented interpretation of synthetic model values, not a claim that uploaded or observed flux has been processed. Real stars require an independently established distance, extinction correction and spectral coverage or an atmosphere-dependent bolometric correction; light alone without these assumptions does not establish intrinsic luminosity. Temperature from color likewise requires extinction and atmosphere assumptions. The spectral index measures relative absorption and is not a disguised stage index.

Selection rule

The candidate set contains every stored, nonrotating, initially solar-composition evolutionary row with a supported atmosphere prediction. Selection minimizes squared distance in logarithmic temperature, logarithmic luminosity, and magnesium wing absorption, each divided by its full candidate-set range. The control being moved has weight 25; the other two have weight one. Exact ties use original source-row order. This is a deterministic interaction rule, not a probability, measurement-error weighting, or physical preference for a phase. Reset selects the nearest model to 5,772 kelvin, one solar luminosity and an illustrative absorption of 0.08 nanometers; these are not measured solar spectral data.

Coverage

Only pre-main sequence, main sequence, post-main sequence including the red giant branch, and core helium burning are included. The source's post-main-sequence flag does not separately identify every subgiant stage. No later phases, white dwarfs, or uncalibrated atmosphere points are fitted. Temperature coverage is within 3,500–7,500 kelvin and requires all atmosphere interpolation corners. Solar composition is a fixed initial iron abundance, not a measurement of surface abundances. Ages and evolutionary physics follow the supplied model grid.

Measured-light diagnostic

The third slider is integrated continuum-normalized magnesium-region wing absorption, in nanometers: the integral of one minus flux divided by continuum over 518.1104–518.3104 and 518.4104–518.6104 nanometers, using air wavelengths in the stellar rest frame. It includes blends and excludes the central line core. It is not a mass, gravity, or phase selector. Its actual predictions retain the existing calibration from 585 published synthetic spectra. Temperature, gravitational acceleration and metallicity are linearly interpolated only within complete atmosphere cells; no extrapolation is allowed.

Atmospheres assume solar-relative carbon and alpha abundances, microturbulence of two kilometers per second and resolving power 10,000. Additional broadening, winds, emission, element-ratio variation, diffusion and differences between atmosphere and evolution mixtures are not fitted. Atmospheric metallicity is assumed equal to initial evolutionary iron abundance. Comparing real spectra requires matching the resolution convention, continuum, wavelength and velocity corrections. These three observables do not generally determine a unique mass or evolutionary stage.

Physics and illustration

Radius follows the Stefan–Boltzmann law. Gravitational acceleration is recomputed from current model mass and that radius, rather than using an independently interpolated source gravity column. Mass is a model output, not a mass–luminosity shortcut. The diagram shows only supported models. Blackbody color and animated surface motion are illustrative; compressed radii are the default, with a true-radius-ratio option. Tiny objects are displayed as markers.
