Assumptions and sources

The controls specify independent illustrative light quantities. They do not snap to a model, and the app does not claim that every combination describes a physically realizable star. Stage remains unresolved because this implementation has no validated observational uncertainty and stage-inference procedure. The magnesium index is retained as a light quantity, but is not currently used to classify the star. The single visualization uses temperature and bolometric luminosity only.

Light-only inputs

The three control quantities are effective temperature inferred from the spectrum or color, intrinsic bolometric luminosity inferred from light with a fixed distance and extinction assumption, and continuum-normalized spectral absorption. No mass, age, gravitational acceleration or evolutionary stage is an input or a hidden slider coordinate. Mass, age and stage are not inferred by this version.

For this illustrative explorer, adopt a fixed fiducial distance of ten parsecs, zero interstellar extinction, isotropic emission and an isolated single star. Intrinsic luminosity equals four times pi times distance squared times extinction-corrected bolometric flux. At this fixed distance each luminosity corresponds to a flux; the slider displays intrinsic luminosity in solar units, never raw apparent flux. This is a documented interpretation of synthetic model values, not a claim that uploaded or observed flux has been processed. Real stars require an independently established distance, extinction correction and spectral coverage or an atmosphere-dependent bolometric correction; light alone without these assumptions does not establish intrinsic luminosity. Temperature from color likewise requires extinction and atmosphere assumptions. The spectral index measures relative absorption and is not a disguised stage index.

No forced classification

No nearest-row selection, phase tie-break, mass estimate or stage estimate is used. The background diagram contains supported evolutionary models for context, not a fitted candidate set. Slider ranges follow that dataset's marginal ranges, not a guarantee of joint compatibility. Reset values are illustrative, not measured solar spectral data. A future classifier must propagate measurement errors and model limitations and preserve ambiguous or out-of-coverage results.

Additional light diagnostics

Bedding and colleagues (2011) demonstrated that gravity-mode period spacings measured from long-duration, high-precision photometry distinguish hydrogen-shell-burning and core-helium-burning red giants in their observed sample. This is evidence for a diagnostic in applicable oscillating red giants, not universal stage classification. It requires identified oscillation modes, sufficient time-series quality and an applicable validated analysis; a single brightness period is not equivalent to mixed-mode period spacing. The present data contain neither mode frequencies nor a validated period-spacing calibration. No seismic slider or numerical stage boundary is fabricated here.

Coverage

Only pre-main sequence, main sequence, post-main sequence including the red giant branch, and core helium burning are included. The source's post-main-sequence flag does not separately identify every subgiant stage. No later phases, white dwarfs, or uncalibrated atmosphere points are fitted. Temperature coverage is within 3,500–7,500 kelvin and requires all atmosphere interpolation corners. Solar composition is a fixed initial iron abundance, not a measurement of surface abundances. Ages and evolutionary physics follow the supplied model grid.

Measured-light diagnostic

The third slider is integrated continuum-normalized magnesium-region wing absorption, in nanometers: the integral of one minus flux divided by continuum over 518.1104–518.3104 and 518.4104–518.6104 nanometers, using air wavelengths in the stellar rest frame. It includes blends and excludes the central line core. It is not a mass, gravity, or phase selector. The background models retain the existing calibration from 585 published synthetic spectra. Temperature, gravitational acceleration and metallicity are linearly interpolated only within complete atmosphere cells; no extrapolation is allowed.

Atmospheres assume solar-relative carbon and alpha abundances, microturbulence of two kilometers per second and resolving power 10,000. Additional broadening, winds, emission, element-ratio variation, diffusion and differences between atmosphere and evolution mixtures are not fitted. Atmospheric metallicity is assumed equal to initial evolutionary iron abundance. Comparing real spectra requires matching the resolution convention, continuum, wavelength and velocity corrections. These three observables do not generally determine a unique mass or evolutionary stage.

Physics and illustration

Radius follows the Stefan–Boltzmann law. Gravitational acceleration is recomputed from current model mass and that radius, rather than using an independently interpolated source gravity column. No mass is displayed. The diagram shows only supported models. Blackbody color and animated surface motion are illustrative; compressed radii are the default, with a true-radius-ratio option. Tiny objects are displayed as markers.
