Real-star test sample

15,000 real Kepler targets, sampled to the published proportions. Random draws uniformly from this fixed database, without checking whether the app fits the star.

Main sequence: 67.33% (10,099). Subgiants: 20.71% (3,107). Giants: 11.96% (1,794). These are Kepler survey proportions, not Galactic population fractions. The source uses broad classes, not uniquely confirmed evolutionary stages.

Measurement coverage

Temperature and radius-derived luminosity: 15,000. Lithium: 71, including six limits. Complete, non-aliased oscillation triples: 259. Compatible magnesium flux indices: zero. No star has all requested measurements.

Missing values remain null. Lithium limits and oscillation aliases are retained in the database but excluded from fitting. Partial oscillation measurements remain visible in the record; the current classifier needs all three. No abundance-to-flux-index substitution or synthetic filling is used.

How to read a comparison

The recorded class is displayed beside, never supplied to, the inference calculation. It does not select, boost or snap the result. Stars outside calibration, tied fits and disagreements stay visible. Selecting a candidate is still your choice. Moving a slider labels the record as exploration instead of continuing to claim it is an unchanged observed-star test.

The app's 3,046 seismic training identifiers are excluded from the draw pool (3,002 occur in this target catalog). Sampling keeps the original catalog class proportions. This avoids direct training-object reuse but is not an independent physical validation: broad reference classes share temperature/radius information, and seismic stages share oscillation evidence. Observational selection and source errors remain.

Luminosity is calculated from published radius and temperature: solar luminosities = solar radii squared × (temperature / 5772 kelvin) to the fourth power. Source radius and temperature uncertainties are retained. The app still matches an exact temperature/luminosity point rather than propagating those uncertainties. Recorded lithium errors replace the exploratory five-milliangstrom error. Oscillation errors are retained but not convolved with the current density estimate. Fit percentages are relative scores, not calibrated probabilities or population estimates.
